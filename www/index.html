Ordering a drink should feel like playing with one.

A mascot-led ordering experience for Teapioca: Pip the cup walks the page, fills with color, shivers, shakes and celebrates while customers build a drink from the real menu board — then walks their order down the track and keeps them playing while they wait.

Why now — the window is open

The official Teapioca Lounge Android app's current average rating — across 94 reviews on its public Google Play listing, last updated November 7, 2026 with "Minor improvements" as the only listed change.

What customers are reporting

  • The app crashing — customers describe it crashing when they try to use it.
  • "No stores available" errors that block ordering before it starts.
  • Repeat orders failing to go through for returning customers.
  • General glitchiness through the ordering flow.

How this concept sidesteps all of it

  • No download: it runs in the phone's browser from a QR code at the counter — nothing to install from an app store.
  • No app-store update: when something needs fixing, the page itself updates — customers never wait on a store release.
  • No store-locator failure mode: the pilot QR sits in one store, so ordering starts already knowing which store it's for.
  • Works on the Android phones customers already carry — the pilot needs no new device and no Play Store listing.

Source: the app's public Google Play listing and its public reviews, checked November 10, 2026. Ratings and review counts change over time. The ask below is unchanged: one store, a short pilot, no POS change.

The problem

  • Ordering is a chore. A wall of menu text, modifiers, and percentages — customers default to "the usual" instead of exploring the board.
  • Waiting is dead time. Five to ten minutes of standing around is where lines feel longest and phones come out — pointed at anything but the brand.
  • Toppings are invisible profit. Add-ons live in a plain list, so most customers never play with them.

The fix: Pip

  • A cup that lives in the page. Pip is a shaded, 3D-look character who strolls the screen, hops onto the menu, carries your step sign, and jumps toward you when something great happens.
  • Every choice is physical. Toppings drop in, ice clinks cube by cube, sweetness pours as a swirl — and you shake the finished drink yourself, just like the counter shakes a BlasTea.
  • The wait becomes play. Pip walks the tracking bar stage by stage, and Boba Catch runs alongside — dead minutes turn into brand time.

Why it wins for Teapioca

  • True to the real menu: the demo is structured on today's board — Cream BlasTea, Art of Tea, Boba Madness, Cub Cup to Jumbo, sweetness to 125% — so staff and customers recognize it instantly.
  • Bigger baskets: toppings become a playful upsell — Pip begs for "just one more," and customers happily add the extras.
  • Repeat visits: kids and families ask for "the cup store." A character who walks your phone is a reason to come back a menu board can't match.
  • Loyalty built in: Genie Points earned in the wait game give regulars a reason to return and redeem.
  • Shorter-feeling waits: a customer playing Boba Catch isn't watching the clock — perceived wait drops even when real wait doesn't.

The pilot proposal

STEP 1

One store, one QR code at the counter and on cups. Mobile web — no app download, no POS change for the pilot.

STEP 2

Run 4–6 weeks. Pip takes demo-style orders that hand off to the existing counter flow.

STEP 3

Measure what matters: average ticket size, toppings per drink, repeat visits, game plays per order.

STEP 4

If the numbers move, expand: official menu, POS and payment integration, more stores, seasonal Pip outfits.

Honest gates before real orders

This demo is a concept. Drink names and categories mirror the current public Teapioca Lounge menu structure; prices are illustrative demo prices, and the demo takes no real orders and no payments. Before anything goes live, it needs:

  • Written brand authorization from Teapioca for the name, menu, and character use
  • The official menu and pricing export, plus store-specific availability
  • POS and payment integration through approved, PCI-compliant providers
  • Final review of taxes, refunds, and pickup workflow with the store team

Those gates are a feature of the pitch, not a footnote — the demo proves the experience first, then earns the integration.
